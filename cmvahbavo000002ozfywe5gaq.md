*By Isaac Muthui, CEO, Tamalaki Business Network Ltd*

The batch has passed its lab checks. The filler is ready. Someone loads a roll of labels onto the machine because "those are the juice labels" and production starts.

An hour later, half the packs carry yesterday's SKU artwork, the wrong net weight, or a date code that does not match the batch record. The product inside is fine. The label is not. That is still a recall risk, a customer complaint and a trust problem—often more expensive than a failed sensory test.

This article is for production supervisors, QA leads, packaging store clerks and owners of small and mid-sized food and agro factories in Kenya: dairies, millers, juice and edible-oil processors, bakeries, snack makers, honey and spice packers, and similar plants. It gives you an eight-point label control checklist to run before you print and pack. It is operations guidance, not legal advice.

## Why label control sits next to quality control

A wrong label does not always look like a quality failure on the day. The pack may seal well, the weight may be correct, and the lab sheet may be green. The failure shows up when a retailer, inspector or consumer compares what the pack *says* with what the pack *is*.

Kenyan and regional public rules make the same point. The [Weights and Measures (Sale and Labelling of Goods) Rules](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/ln/1999/24/eng@2022-12-31) require pre-packed goods to show the manufacturer's (or responsible packer's) name and address, the common or generic product name, and net quantity, in English and/or Kiswahili and readable on the principal display panel. Food labelling rules under the Food, Drugs and Chemical Substances framework likewise expect a common name, ingredients where applicable, and manufacturer or packer details. Draft [DEAS 38:2024 on labelling of pre-packaged foods](https://www.kebs.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/DEAS-38-2024-Labelling-and-pre-packaged-foods-Requirements.pdf) (KEBS public review) emphasises lot identification, country of origin and manufacturer address. The USDA [FAIRS Country Report for Kenya](https://apps.fas.usda.gov/newgainapi/api/Report/DownloadReportByFileName?fileName=FAIRS+Country+Report+Annual_Nairobi_Kenya_KE2024-0004.pdf) summarises KEBS-led practice for consumer-ready foods along the same lines: language, common name, ingredients order, date marking, metric net content, manufacturer details and country of origin.

None of that replaces your counsel or a formal food safety plan. It supports a floor rule: **treat the label as part of the product—do not start packing until this run's label is right.**

KEBS [Standardization Mark](https://qualityassurance.kebs.go.ke/standardization-mark/) work and the [STA-10 initial inspection form](https://kebs.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/STA-10-Initial-inspection-report.pdf) look at process and quality records; the MoH [Food Hygiene Licensing Checklist (April 2023)](https://health.go.ke/sites/default/files/2023-05/Checklist%20for%20Food%20Hygiene%20Licensing%20April%202023.pdf) expects documented process controls. Label mix-ups are identity hazards, not only marketing issues. They also overlap [line clearance](https://factoraerp.com/en/blog/line-clearance-checklist-food-factories-kenya): leftover rolls from the last SKU are a common failure mode.

## The 8-point label control checklist

Run these checks before the pack run starts at speed. Keep answers on paper or in a system so you can show them later.

### 1\. Is this the approved artwork / label version for this SKU?

Confirm you are not printing from memory or from "the roll that was nearest":

- The label or artwork revision matches the current approved version for this product and pack size.
- Marketing, regulatory or customer-driven changes (new claim, new address, new allergen statement) are on the version you are about to use.
- Obsolete artwork is marked obsolete and kept away from the line.

A beautiful label that is one revision behind is still the wrong label.

### 2\. Are the mandatory particulars present and readable?

Before you commit a full roll, check that the label carries the particulars your product type requires under Kenyan labelling practice, including as summarised above:

- Common or generic name of the food.
- Name and address of the manufacturer, packer or responsible seller.
- Net quantity in metric units, clearly presented.
- Date marking (manufacture and/or expiry or sell-by, as your product requires).
- Language: English and/or Kiswahili, legible on the principal display panel where rules require it.
- Ingredients list in order of predominance where the product type requires it.

If a mandatory field is missing, blurred or cut off by the die, stop. Do not "run and fix the next print job."

### 3\. Does the ingredients list (and any allergen statement) match the current recipe?

The label must describe what is in the hopper today:

- Ingredients and allergens match the current approved recipe or bill of materials (BOM) for this SKU.
- Reformulations (new sweetener, new oil, new flavour, milk powder added or removed) have a matching label version.
- "May contain" or similar statements, if you use them, match your own rules—not last year's template.

A correct process with an outdated ingredients panel is still a mislabel.

### 4\. Does the declared net quantity match the pack and the fill target?

Weights and measures failures often start as packaging shortcuts:

- Declared net weight or volume matches the pack size you are filling.
- Fill targets and checkweigher or volume checks are set for that declaration.
- Multi-packs and inner packs tell a consistent story (unit size × count = declared total where that is how you sell).

Wrong net quantity is not a small print error. It is a compliance and customer-trust problem.

### 5\. Are lot / batch identity and date codes set for *this* production run?

Every packed unit should be traceable:

- The batch or lot code on the coder matches the production batch you are packing.
- Manufacture and expiry (or best-before / sell-by) dates match your shelf-life rules for this product and run.
- A sample print is readable after coding (ink, stamp or thermal) before the line runs at speed.

Without correct lot and date coding, a later [mock recall](https://factoraerp.com/en/blog/how-to-run-a-mock-recall-food-traceability-kenya) cannot find the right packs. Coding also feeds [FEFO and expiry control](https://factoraerp.com/en/blog/reduce-expiry-spoilage-losses-fefo-batch-expiry-tracking) once goods sit in the warehouse.

### 6\. Are manufacturer details and country of origin correct for this product?

Identity on the pack must match who is responsible for the food:

- Manufacturer or packer name and address match your current legal / commercial details (or the brand owner's agreed details if you contract-pack).
- Country of origin is declared correctly for the product as sold.
- Where you pack for another brand, the label still identifies the responsible packer or seller as your agreements and the applicable rules require.

Draft EAS 38-style expectations around lot ID, origin and manufacturer address exist to support traceability—not decoration.

### 7\. Has the correct physical label stock been issued to the line—and have leftovers been removed?

This is where many Kenyan pack rooms fail on busy changeovers:

- Label stock issued from the store matches the production order SKU and artwork version (count the rolls; check the SKU or job code on the carton).
- Leftover labels, sleeves, lids and cartons from the previous SKU are removed from the line, including partial rolls and "spares under the machine."
- Returned unused labels go back under their identity, not into a mixed "labels" shelf.

This check is the packaging twin of [line clearance](https://factoraerp.com/en/blog/line-clearance-checklist-food-factories-kenya). If the wrong roll can sit on a spare spindle, it will eventually be used.

### 8\. Has first-off print and pack verification passed—and is the decision recorded?

Do not trust the set-up alone. Confirm with product:

- Print and inspect first-off packs: artwork, mandatory text, net quantity, lot/date code, seal and appearance.
- Hold those packs until they pass. If they fail, stop, correct, scrap or quarantine misprinted stock, and recheck.
- Record who checked, which SKU and batch, and the accept / rework / scrap decision before full speed.

Finished packed goods should still pass your normal release step, such as a [QA hold before the batch leaves the warehouse](https://factoraerp.com/en/blog/qa-hold-checklist-before-batch-leaves-warehouse-kenya). Label control at the line reduces how often the warehouse has to catch a misprint that should never have been packed.

## Habits that make label control stick

- **Issue labels per production order.** Do not leave open rolls of multiple SKUs at the same line.
- **Separate set-up from check.** The person who loads the roll or coder should not be the only first-off signer.
- **Quarantine bad prints.** Misprints are not nearly-finished goods; keep them away from released stock.
- **Version artwork like a recipe.** When recipe or legal text changes, obsolete the old label the same day.
- **Tie packaging to intake and invoices.** Labels arrive through [supplier intake](https://factoraerp.com/en/blog/supplier-intake-checklist-food-manufacturers-kenya); stock and invoices increasingly need to align with [eTIMS](https://factoraerp.com/en/blog/etims-for-food-agro-manufacturers-kenya).

**Illustration (not real customer data):** a juice plant finishes mango nectar and switches to orange. The filler is cleaned, but a half-used mango label roll stays on the spare spindle. After lunch the orange run starts; when the first roll empties, someone mounts the spare. Two pallets leave as orange under mango artwork. A five-minute check—only this SKU's labels on the line, first-off matches the order—would have caught it.

## FAQ

**What is a label control checklist in a food factory?**\
It is a short, repeatable set of checks run before and at the start of packing to confirm the correct approved artwork, mandatory label particulars, ingredients match to recipe, net quantity, lot/date coding, manufacturer and origin details, correct label stock on the line, and a recorded first-off verification.

**Who should sign off first-off packs?**\
Usually a named QA lead or packaging supervisor with authority to stop the line. The person who mounted the label roll or set the coder should not be the only person who approves the first packs.

**What should happen to misprinted packs?**\
Quarantine and clearly mark them. Record the reason, scrap or rework under your procedure, and do not mix them with released finished goods.

**How is this different from line clearance?**\
Line clearance clears the previous product, residue, WIP and packaging. Label control confirms identity for *this* pack run. Use both; leftover rolls are a shared failure mode.

**Can a small factory do this without software?**\
Yes. Approved artwork, labelled shelves for label stock, a printed checklist, a first-off sheet and named owners cover the basics. Software helps when SKUs and versions outgrow paper.
